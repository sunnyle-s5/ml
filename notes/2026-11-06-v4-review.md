## 1 · Bottom line

- **v4 ≈ v3: it is not better enough to replace v3.** By v3's own rule, one list beats another only by ≥ 0.2 pt
  (`RULE_MARGIN_PT`, `experiments/2026-11-05-v3-unified/run.py`), and v4 misses that.
  - Its top 10 reaches 93.26 % HR@10: +1.21 pt over v1, and only +0.08 pt over v3's 93.18 %.
  - It is better than v3 on 17/20 clusters (range −0.48 to +0.44 pt).
  - Its 3/5 form is 0.03 pt below v3's.
  - The extra cost: a 60-day market-cache read, two candidate sources and 8 features in every build.
- **The research's best offline levers are already in v3.** Learning which own games come back is 76 % of v4's feature
  gain (play days, days ratio, recency).
  - The new candidate sources add +1.61 pt of candidate recall for new games.
  - The ranker turns that into +0.08 pt HR@10 and +0.10 pt adopted-game HR.
- **No version passes the discovery track** (+0.3 pt adopted-game HR). Weighting the ranker's labels by play days
  (Netflix Exp-RSFT) changes adopted-game HR by ±0.01 pt. Discovery needs online data: the exploration slot and nudges,
  issues 06 and 07.
- **For the POC: keep the v3 list and the v3 cold-player fallback.**
  - Treatment: unified top 10, or learned continue if Product keeps discovery. That is the only form that passes the HR@10
    track with new-game HR held.
  - What v4 adds for the POC is measurement: issue 08, the anytime-valid guardrail tests, a popularity-tier metric and
    the rollout analysis. These are for decision.
